## 6. Gaps: evidence and guidelines the course leaves out

1. **Current suicide-risk guidance.** NICE NG225 (2022) says: "Do not use risk assessment tools and scales to predict future suicide or repetition of self-harm", and do not use global low/medium/high stratification. It recommends needs-based assessment and risk formulation instead. See also Franklin et al. (2017), *Psychological Bulletin*, 143(2), 187–232.
2. **ISSTD guidelines.** "Guidelines for Treating Dissociative Identity Disorder in Adults, Third Revision" (2011), *Journal of Trauma & Dissociation*, 12(2), 115–187. This is the field's treatment guideline, it is still the current revision, and Leeds (p. 131) tells readers to review it. The course never cites it.
3. **The debate over phase-based treatment for CPTSD.** De Jongh et al. (2016), *Depression and Anxiety*, 33(5), 359–369, argue that the evidence for mandatory stabilization in CPTSD is methodologically limited. Cloitre (2016) replied in the same journal. The course teaches stabilization-first without mentioning this debate. For DID, phase-oriented treatment remains the ISSTD consensus.
4. **The progressive approach.** Gonzalez & Mosquera (2012) bring modified EMDR into the preparation phase. Leeds's glossary (p. 449) presents it as an alternative to the ISSTD model.
5. **Better screening tools.** DES-Taxon (Waller & Ross, 1997) and SDQ-20 or SDQ-5 (Nijenhuis et al., 1996, 1997) for somatoform dissociation, which the DES-II misses (Leeds pp. 130, 134).
6. **ICD-11 6B63, possession trance disorder.** This matters for Arab clients who describe experiences as jinn possession, and for cultural formulation in general.
7. **Arabic-validated instruments.** The course doesn't say whether the DES-II, MID or TADS-I have validated Arabic versions. I did not search for these; that needs a literature check.
8. **RDI cautions.** Patients with dismissing or fearful attachment may respond poorly (Leeds p. 161). Clinicians sometimes overuse RDI because of their own avoidance (Leeds p. 162), which fits the "therapist contribution" theme of block 2.5.
9. **Denial of diagnosis as a reason to postpone** reprocessing (Leeds p. 128).

## 7. Teaching quality

**Structure: strong.**
- 20 blocks of 60 minutes each. Every block's timing table adds up to exactly 60 (checked).
- The sequence runs: explanation, then skill, then worked example, then case task, then summary.
- About 484 of 1,200 minutes (40%) are lecture; the rest is modelling, tasks and discussion.
- Two continuing cases are revealed step by step (Reem R1–R12, Tarek T1–T12). The case record controls what is revealed when, and it is consistent with the decks.
- Content warnings, check-in pauses, a designated assistant for participant distress, and minute-by-minute timings for each task are strengths.

**Learning objectives: mostly good.**
- There are 42 objectives. Most use observable verbs: distinguish, classify, formulate, decide, choose, justify.
- Four use verbs that can't be measured:
  - 2.2 "understand the function of avoidance" (تفهم)
  - 2.4 "become familiar with the language of collaboration" (تتعرّف)
  - 2.5 "become familiar with your contribution" (تتعرّف)
  - 4.4 "know the indications" (تعرف)
- Rewrite these as observable behaviour, for example "name the probable function of an avoidance behaviour in a case vignette". Continuing-education accreditors usually require measurable objectives; check the current EMDRIA credit criteria.

**How well assessments match objectives: partial.**
- Most objectives have a matching written task (Tasks 1–21).
- Three objectives have no task that assesses them:
  - 1.3 "explain action systems, ANP/EP and the three levels". Task 2 tests only generating a question.
  - 2.4 "explore the function of the part that wants to die". No task covers it.
  - 3.1 "build a small AIP map". It is assessed only by Task 11, which the manual marks optional and cut if time is short.
- The 12-item pre-test/post-test cannot cover 42 objectives, and it was not supplied, so its alignment could not be assessed.
- The course measures knowledge change, not changes in clinical behaviour.

**Practical application: appropriate for the course's stated aim, and should be stated clearly.**
- All tasks are individual written case decisions. Demonstrations are read aloud by the trainer, and there is no bilateral stimulation and no practice in pairs.
- This matches the course's stated aim of teaching decision-making, with specialized procedures covered only as indications and limits.
- Certificates and marketing should say explicitly that the course does not confer competence in CIPOS, Loving Eyes, RDI, Flash or work with parts. The Day 4 closing slide already says these need separate training.

**Language consistency.** The trainer manual and workbook are in Modern Standard Arabic, while the slides and speaker notes are in Egyptian colloquial. This seems intentional, since the manual says there is a colloquial version of it. Confirm it suits a pan-Arab audience.

## 8. Where evidence is thin or conflicting

- **Predicting suicide.** The course follows Leeds (2016), but the later meta-analytic evidence and NICE guidance disagree with Leeds (see #19 and #22). This is a genuine conflict between sources, not a minor update.
- **RDI** has no high-quality controlled trial of the full protocol (Leeds p. 162). Hornsveld et al. (2011) called eye movements in RDI "questionable", and Leeds & Korn (2012) rejected that critique. The course should present it as contested.
- **Flash.** One RCT in a non-clinical population. The PTSD RCT (ENHANCE) had no published results in my search.
- **The DES-II** misses many dissociative disorders (Leeds p. 130).
- **The TADS-I** has only pilot validation (2025).
- **The structural dissociation model** is a theoretical model. Its levels are clinical constructs, not validated diagnostic categories. The trauma origin of dissociation is itself debated: Dalenberg et al. (2012) argue for it, and Lynn et al. (2014), *Psychological Bulletin*, 140(3), 896–910, argue it is overstated. Even the TTRD authors disagree about the ANP/EP terms (TTRD ch. 1). The course frames the model as "a tool for generating a question, not for classifying", which is the right stance. The spectrum slide (#7) is the one place it slips.
- **Phase-based treatment for CPTSD** is contested (De Jongh et al., 2016, versus Cloitre, 2016). For DID, phase-oriented treatment is consensus (ISSTD, 2011), but that consensus is expert-driven rather than based on RCTs.
